# Business Benchmark on Farm Animal Welfare (BBFAW) — 2024

## What BBFAW Is

The Business Benchmark on Farm Animal Welfare is an independent annual benchmark assessing corporate practices and reporting on farm animal welfare. Now in its 13th year (2024 report launched March 27, 2025). Covers 150 of the world's largest food companies across 51 distinct, objective criteria.

Described as "the most authoritative and comprehensive global account of corporate practice on farm animal welfare."

## Purpose & Stakeholders

- **For investors:** Information to integrate farm animal welfare into investment decisions; 32 investor signatories + 30 collaborating investors
- **For companies:** Clear expectations for management practices and performance standards vs. peers
- **For advocates:** Data on which companies are leading vs. lagging, and the gap between commitments and implementation

## Scoring & Tier System

Companies receive percentage scores placed into 6 tiers:

| Tier | Score Range | Description |
|------|------------|-------------|
| Tier 1 | >80% | Leadership position achieved |
| Tier 2 | 62–80% | Welfare integrated into business strategy |
| Tier 3 | 44–61% | Established approach needing better implementation |
| Tier 4 | 27–43% | Making progress on policy implementation |
| Tier 5 | 11–26% | Limited evidence of effective management |
| Tier 6 | <11% | Minimal recognition of farm animal welfare |

## 2024 Key Findings

- **Average score: 18%** across all 150 assessed companies
- **Top performers (Tier 2):** Greggs, Marks & Spencer, Premier Foods, Waitrose — only 4 companies reached Tier 2
- **95% of companies** address importance of animal welfare with policy commitments, but majority are not yet implementing
- **52% of companies** have NO policy to manage routine mutilations (branding, tail docking)
- 14 companies moved up a tier; 14 more improved their Impact Rating
- **Key gap:** "Progress on commitments but lack of implementation" — the headline finding

## Methodology Note

In 2021, BBFAW updated methodology to emphasize "performance reporting and performance impact" rather than just policy statements. This caused average scores to drop from 35% (2020) to 32% (2021) — 36 companies dropped tiers. Illustrates that measuring actual implementation vs. stated commitment changes the picture significantly.

## Data Coverage

- 150 global food companies assessed
- Annual benchmark since 2012
