# Feasible Transformative AI × Animals Interventions

Author's caveat: "nothing emerged as strongly compelling." The uncertainty centers on whether any intervention robustly improves animal outcomes given transformative AI's speculative nature.

---

## POSITIVELY / NEUTRALLY ASSESSED INTERVENTIONS

### 1. Epistemic and Coordination Tools
**What:** Build tools to improve human decision-making, deliberation, and compromise. Theory: better epistemic environments surface latent preferences for animal welfare.
**Feasibility:** Tools can be developed; causal chains are long and conjunctive.
**Assessment:** Conditional on slower AI takeoff; unclear real-world impact.

### 2. Preventing/Improving Space Terraforming Processes
**What:** Advocate for space governance norms preserving space from Earth-originating biological life.
**Feasibility:** Marginal advisory influence on discourse.
**Assessment:** Ambitious; unlikely traction; risk of backfire.

### 3. Seizing Opportunities in Current Priority Areas
**What:** Pivot existing farmed animal welfare, alt protein, or wild animal welfare programs toward TAI-specific opportunities; or dedicate one researcher per area.
**Feasibility:** Could leverage AI for faster feedback loops in alt protein R&D.
**Assessment:** Upsides potentially substantial; feedback loops remain problematic.

### 4. Improving AW Perception in Influential Tech Spaces
**What:** Build credibility within tech circles through high-signal engagements; develop professional, transpartisan animal welfare messaging.
**Example:** Lewis Bollard's Dwarkesh podcast appearance as model.
**Feasibility:** Identified opportunities exist; scalable within tech communities.
**Assessment:** Doesn't require TAI belief for support; few good opportunities available.

### 5. AI Pause / Anti-AI Advocacy
**What:** Promote TAI development pauses, superintelligence bans, or strict development red lines.
**Feasibility:** Groups like Pause AI actively recruit; some AI governance orgs pursue this.
**Assessment:** Depends entirely on whether TAI restraint is realistically achievable — highly contested.

### 6. Supporting Broad AI Safety Efforts
**What:** Contribute to mainstream AI safety work to ensure TAI development proceeds safely.
**Feasibility:** Scalable; many animal advocates already pursue this.
**Assessment:** High feasibility; benefits depend on whether AI safety outcomes benefit non-human animals.

### 7. Preventing Value Lock-In and Takeovers
**What:** Prevent status quo values (poor animal treatment) from becoming permanently locked into AI systems or concentrated power structures.
**Organizations:** Forethought, Macroscopic.
**Assessment:** Difficult to assess; judgments on lock-in often "rash"; high implementation risk.

### 8. Integrating AW into AI Governance
**What:** Lobby for inclusion of sentient nonhuman interests within intergovernmental frameworks and codes of practice.
**Example:** EU AI Code of Practice inclusion — at least one documented success.
**Feasibility:** Few commitments exist; could be pursued by pre-existing organizations.
**Assessment:** Risk of appearing as narrow interest group; potential dilution of broader lobbying.

### 9. Corporate Commitments and Benchmarks for AI Labs
**What:** Encourage AI labs to explicitly include "sentient beings" in training specifications, mission statements, and system "constitutions."
**Who:** Sentient Futures exploring this; researchers appear receptive.
**Feasibility:** Scale existing organizations or launch focused 1–2 person teams.
**Assessment:** Some labs receptive; securing concrete success remains difficult.

### 10. Targeted Training Data
**What:** Generate synthetic pretraining data oriented toward animal consideration.
**Example:** Compassion in Machine Learning (CaML) — uses "synthetic data oriented toward consideration for animals."
**Evidence:** Experimental evidence shows improved animal-compassion scores in open-weight models with persistence after fine-tuning.
**Feasibility:** Already underway; passably likely labs adopt such data.
**Assessment:** Strongest evidence base among all listed interventions.

### 11. Animal-Inclusive Alignment Research
**What:** Launch technical/foundational research org identifying animal-harming tendencies in AI systems; research robustness of "alignment to all sentient beings."
**Feasibility:** Hardest to launch — requires competitive salaries, hub position, faces timeline pressure.
**Assessment:** No clear progress track record; foundational work harder with short timeline fears.

---

## NEGATIVELY ASSESSED INTERVENTIONS

### 12. Moral Circle Expansion via Animal Communication
**Assessment:** Likely inevitable anyway; hard to scale concern to smaller animals; animal communication may worsen small animal replacement problem.

### 13. Patient Philanthropy Fund for TAIA
**Assessment:** Extremely low feasibility; no track record for knowing when to act; benefits flow to conventional welfare if TAI never materializes.

### 14. Sparking Public Discourse on AI-Driven Animal Harm
**Assessment:** Highly likely to backfire; low-fidelity messaging; current frontier models already somewhat animal-friendly.

### 15. Searching for "Cause X" Within TAI×Animals
**Assessment:** No track record finding actionable causes "from scratch"; implementation infrastructure uncertain.

---

## Cross-Cutting Observation

The most tractable near-term interventions (#4, #8, #9, #10) share a common structure: **work within existing AI development processes** rather than trying to redirect or stop them. The interventions with the strongest evidence base (#10 — targeted training data) are also the ones most disconnected from the traditional AW advocacy toolkit.
