# BBFAW 2024

**Type:** Annual benchmark report  
**Author:** Business Benchmark on Farm Animal Welfare  
**Published:** March 2025 (2024 data)  
**Raw file:** [[raw/BBFAW 2024 Overview]]  
**URL:** https://www.bbfaw.com

## Summary

The 13th annual BBFAW assesses 150 global food companies on 51 criteria across farm animal welfare management and reporting. The headline finding: 95% of companies have welfare policies, but the average score is only 18% — a massive gap between stated commitment and actual implementation. This is the existing infrastructure for corporate welfare monitoring, and it reveals both what's possible and where the gaps are.

## Key Points

- **18% average score** across 150 companies — most companies are at Tier 5-6 (minimal to limited evidence of effective management)
- **Only 4 companies** reached Tier 2 (Greggs, M&S, Premier Foods, Waitrose) — all UK-based; no US, Asian, or Latin American companies in the top tier
- **The commitment-implementation gap is the defining finding:** 95% have policies; 18% average score means policies aren't translating to practice
- **52% of companies** have no policy on routine mutilations — a basic welfare issue
- **Methodology tightening matters:** When BBFAW shifted to measuring actual performance (not just policy statements) in 2021, average scores dropped from 35% to 32% and 36 companies fell a tier. This is the right direction for MEL.
- **Coverage:** 150 companies, annual since 2012, publicly available, used by 32+ institutional investors

## What BBFAW Does Well (Existing Infrastructure)

- Annual, consistent, public dataset on corporate welfare
- Tier system that enables year-over-year tracking
- Investor integration — 32 signatories use it for ESG decisions
- Covers both policy and (increasingly) implementation

## What BBFAW Doesn't Do (The Gap)

- Only covers the 150 largest global food companies — misses thousands of mid-size and regional companies
- Annual cadence — can't detect within-year compliance lapses or backsliding
- UK/European bias in methodology development and top performers
- Relies on company self-reporting + public disclosure, not independent verification
- Cannot verify what actually happens on farms — only what companies report
- Does not cover supply chain tiers below the direct company
