# AI Can Help Animal Advocacy More Than Industrial Farming

**Type:** EA Forum post  
**Author:** Anonymous (EA Forum)  
**Published:** 2024  
**Raw file:** [[raw/EA Forum - AI Can Help Animal Advocacy]]  
**URL:** https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/XHeihQrKjrLEdAdNM/

## Summary

Argues that AI will benefit animal welfare movements more than it benefits industrial farming, because advocacy is fundamentally an information problem (AI's strength) while intensive farming is constrained by biology and physics (which AI can't overcome). Proposes transparency legislation as the most politically viable near-term intervention.

## Key Points

- **The asymmetry:** Industrial farming's biggest constraints are biological (animals need time to grow, land and water are finite). AI optimizes within these constraints but can't break them. Advocacy's biggest constraints are information and capacity — exactly what AI addresses.
- **Five advocacy applications:** data analysis for consumers, documenting suffering, resource efficiency for underfunded orgs, alternative protein acceleration, transparency monitoring of farms and supply chains.
- **Resource efficiency is underappreciated:** AI automating labor-intensive tasks matters most for small, underfunded AW organizations — especially those in the Global South where most production occurs.
- **Cautionary example:** AI-powered ultra-intensive farming (26-story pig farms in China) shows AI *can* help industrial farming scale up — the asymmetry is about net effect, not absolute help.
- **Policy proposal:** Transparency legislation requiring farm data to be accessible to independent stakeholders — more politically feasible than technology bans, aligns with consumer, public health, and environmental coalitions.
- **Counterargument noted:** AI welfare improvements may enable *more* intensification (disease prevention → higher stocking density). Historical precedent: antibiotics enabled overcrowding.
