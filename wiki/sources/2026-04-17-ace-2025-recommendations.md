# ACE 2025 Charity Recommendations

**Type:** Annual report / evaluation  
**Author:** Animal Charity Evaluators (ACE)  
**Published:** 2025  
**Raw file:** [[raw/ACE 2025 Recommendations]]  
**URL:** https://animalcharityevaluators.org/blog/announcing-our-2025-charity-recommendations/

## Summary

ACE's 2025 list of 10 recommended animal welfare charities, with evaluation summaries for each. Covers a diverse set of intervention types (corporate campaigns, plant-based food systems, wild animal research, aquatic animal welfare) across multiple countries. Includes cost-effectiveness estimates. Primary value for the wiki: gives a concrete picture of who is doing what in the AW sector, what outcomes look like, and where monitoring gaps exist.

## Key Points

- **Corporate cage-free campaigns dominate** the recommended list — The Humane League, Sinergia Animal, ÇHKD, Animal Welfare Observatory all run corporate commitment campaigns. This confirms corporate pledge monitoring as a high-priority opportunity.
- **Global South coverage is growing** — Brazil (SVB), China (Good Food Fund), Turkey (ÇHKD), Latin America + Southeast Asia (Sinergia Animal). Most production happens here; MEL capacity is thinnest here.
- **Aquatic animals are rising** — Shrimp Welfare Project and Aquatic Life Institute both on the list. Shrimp welfare is notable: 1.74 billion shrimps covered by commitments, at potentially enormous scale.
- **Wild animal welfare research is funded but sparse** — WAI awarded $4.7M to 72 projects. This is the entire field's research budget.
- **Cost-effectiveness is tracked but rough** — "Three hens per dollar" for ÇHKD; "thousands of shrimps per dollar" for ALI. These are estimates, not rigorous measurements. Illustrates the MEL gap: orgs can estimate cost-effectiveness but struggle to verify actual welfare change.
- **Scale:** $58M+ influenced in donations since 2019; 167 recipient orgs; 42 countries.

## Evaluation Methodology (ACE)

ACE assesses charities on:
- Theory of change validity
- Organizational health metrics
- Financial responsibility
- Future funding plans and impact potential
- Cost-effectiveness of interventions

**MEL observation:** ACE's methodology is essentially a MEL framework applied to AW charities. Their evaluations reveal common gaps: most orgs track outputs (pledges, commitments) rather than outcomes (actual welfare improvement for animals).
