# Integrating Animal Welfare into AI Governance

**Problem:** Current AI governance frameworks (EU AI Act, US EO on AI, international standards bodies) are almost entirely human-centered. Animal interests are invisible in the documents that will shape how AI systems are built and deployed for decades.  
**AI Solution:** This is an advocacy intervention *about* AI, not an AI tool — lobbying and expert engagement to get "sentient beings" rather than "humans" into foundational AI governance documents, codes of practice, and AI lab mission statements.  
**Target Orgs:** Sentient Futures (actively pursuing this), existing AW policy orgs with EU/international presence, AI governance orgs open to broader sentience framing.  
**Status:** idea  
**Feasibility:** Medium — at least one documented success (EU AI Code of Practice); the window is now, as frameworks are still being written.

## Problem Description

The governance frameworks being written right now — the EU AI Act, national AI strategies, voluntary codes of practice for AI labs, international AI standards — will shape how AI is developed and what values it embeds for a very long time. These documents define whose interests AI should protect, what harms it should avoid, and what "beneficial AI" means.

Currently, "beneficial AI" = beneficial to humans. Animals are at best mentioned as "livestock" in economic contexts or as test subjects to be protected from research harm. The vast majority of animals — the billions in factory farms, the trillions of wild animals — are completely invisible.

If these frameworks get locked in with human-only framing, it becomes structurally harder for AI systems to reason about animal welfare, for AI labs to justify animal welfare as a mission consideration, and for future governance reforms to include animals.

## The Window

AI governance frameworks are unusually malleable *right now*:
- EU AI Code of Practice is still being drafted (as of 2024-2025)
- Many AI labs are writing or revising their model specifications ("constitutions")
- International AI governance bodies (UN, GPAI, etc.) are establishing their scope
- Norms are not yet set

This is a narrow window. Once locked in, these frameworks will be extremely difficult to change.

## Proposed Approach

**Level 1 — AI Lab Constitutions and Specs:**  
Engage AI labs (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, etc.) to include "sentient beings" rather than "humans" in their model specifications, mission statements, and safety frameworks. Sentient Futures is actively pursuing this.

**Level 2 — Voluntary Codes of Practice:**  
Participate in multi-stakeholder AI governance processes (EU AI Code of Practice, industry self-regulation bodies) as an expert voice on animal sentience and welfare.

**Level 3 — Intergovernmental Frameworks:**  
Longer-term: engage UN, OECD AI Principles, and other intergovernmental bodies to include non-human sentient interests in their scope.

## Evidence of Feasibility

- **EU AI Code of Practice success:** At least one documented case of AW content being included (source: TAIA interventions post)
- **AI lab receptiveness:** Researchers at major labs appear receptive to "sentient beings" framing per Sentient Futures' engagement
- **Precedent in other domains:** Environmental interests were successfully embedded in trade agreements, financial regulations, and international standards over time

## Risks

- **Narrow interest group perception:** AW advocates engaging on AI governance may be seen as capturing the process for a niche concern, reducing their effectiveness and potentially harming broader governance goals
- **Dilution:** "Sentient beings" language may get watered down to meaninglessness in final documents
- **Crowding out:** AW orgs pushing on AI governance may crowd out each other or conflict with AI safety advocates who have different priorities

## Relevance to AW/MEL

This is hard to evaluate with standard MEL approaches (outcomes are diffuse, attribution is unclear, timelines are long). A good MEL framework for this intervention would track: number of governance documents that include sentient beings language, quality of language (binding vs. aspirational), AI lab policy changes, and downstream effects on AI system behavior.
