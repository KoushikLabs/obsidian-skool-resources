# Wild Animal Welfare

## Definition

The field concerned with the welfare of animals living in the wild — not in farms or captivity. Motivated by the recognition that wild animals vastly outnumber farmed animals, and many wild lives may involve significant suffering (predation, disease, parasites, starvation, weather). A relatively young and contested field.

## Key Debates

- **Scope:** How much suffering exists in wild animal populations? R-strategists (many offspring, most die young) vs. K-strategists (few offspring, long lives).
- **Intervention ethics:** Is it appropriate to intervene in wild ecosystems to reduce suffering? How do you avoid making things worse?
- **Tractability:** Most wild animal suffering happens at scales and in contexts where intervention is currently impossible. Near-term focus tends to be on proximate harms humans cause (pest control, habitat destruction, wildlife culling).
- **Sentience:** Which wild animals are sentient? Insects? Fish? Crustacea? This determines the moral weight of interventions.

## Key Intervention Types

- Ending or humanizing human-caused wild animal deaths (rodenticide, culling, bycatch)
- Wildlife contraception programs
- Wildlife disease treatment/vaccination
- Welfare considerations in conservation (conservation ≠ welfare — conflicts exist)
- Research and advocacy on wild animal sentience

## MEL Challenges

- Scale makes direct welfare measurement nearly impossible
- Long time horizons and diffuse causality
- Difficult to define "welfare improvement" at population level
- Research-to-practice gap is especially large — most orgs are doing advocacy/research, not direct delivery

## Relevance to AI Opportunities

- **Monitoring:** Remote sensing + computer vision for wildlife population welfare indicators
- **Research synthesis:** AI-assisted literature reviews on sentience, intervention effects
- **Disease surveillance:** Early warning systems for wildlife disease outbreaks
- **Scale estimation:** AI modeling of wild animal population distributions and suffering estimates
