# US-China AI Race

## Definition

The geopolitical competition between the United States and China to develop advanced AI first, particularly AGI and superintelligence. The race is primarily a compute competition (who has more AI-relevant hardware) combined with an algorithms competition (who has better training methods). It shapes national security strategy, chip policy, and corporate governance of AI labs.

## Key Asymmetries (per AI 2027 scenario, late 2027)

| Dimension | US (OpenBrain) | China (DeepCent) |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------|
| Share of world AI compute | ~20% (OpenBrain alone); ~70% all US companies | ~10–12% |
| Algorithmic R&D speed | 25x human baseline | 10x human baseline |
| Lag behind frontier | — | ~2 months |
| Centralization | Distributed across companies | ~50% at single CDZ (Tianwan nuclear plant) |
| Chip supply | TSMC frontier chips | Smuggled/older chips, domestic chips ~3yr behind |

China's centralization is both a strength (concentration of resources) and a vulnerability (single-point target for cyberattacks or kinetic strikes).

## Key Events in the Scenario

- **Chip export controls:** US restricts sale of frontier AI chips to China from 2024 onward. China responds by smuggling Taiwanese chips and building domestic alternatives.
- **China nationalizes AI (mid-2026):** CCP creates the Centralized Development Zone (CDZ) at Tianwan nuclear plant, merging all major AI research into a DeepCent-led collective.
- **Agent-2 theft (February 2027):** Chinese intelligence steals OpenBrain's Agent-2 weights through a coordinated insider attack — fast (~2 hours), using multiple servers exfiltrating 100GB chunks simultaneously.
- **US retaliates:** Cyberattacks against CDZ largely fail because China has airgapped it. Military assets reposition around Taiwan. Wiretapping catches the last US-side spy.
- **Taiwan as the wildcard:** TSMC manufactures >80% of US AI chips. Hawks in China discuss blockade or invasion as a way to cut off US chip supply. Never executed in the scenario — but treated as a live option.
- **Arms control talks go nowhere:** US sees unilateral compute advantage as preferable to any treaty.

## Structural Dynamic

The race creates a *safety-capability tradeoff* at every decision point:
- Every time the safety team wants to pause over alignment concerns, the response is "DeepCent is two months behind."
- The government can't pause OpenBrain without either sabotaging China (kinetic strikes) or negotiating a treaty (politically infeasible).
- This dynamic is why the "slowdown" ending is described as hard — it requires either a breakthrough in trust between the US and China, or a catastrophic AI incident that makes both sides blink.

## Tensions & Open Questions

- Does the compute gap actually translate to a capability gap, given China's algorithmic talent? The scenario says yes, but with uncertainty.
- Is Taiwan the real choke point? If so, chip policy and Taiwan defense are effectively AI policy.
- Could a third party (EU, India) emerge as a significant actor? The scenario largely ignores this.
