How an 18-Year-Old Built a $100M AI App This guide breaks down the exact strategies behind Cal AI's rise from a simple idea to a nine-figure acquisition, and how you can follow the same three-step playbook to build your own AI app in 2026.

By: Nate Herk The Cal AI Story In December 2025, 18-year-old Zach sold his AI-powered calorie tracking app, Cal AI, for what experts estimate could be over $100 million.

The app had just finished 2025 generating $30 million in annual revenue and ranked among the top health and fitness apps in Apple's App Store.

What makes this story remarkable is how it started. There were no investors, no fancy office, and no highly trained development team.

Zach built the app with his high school friends and scaled it in roughly one year.

The playbook he followed can be broken down into three repeatable steps.

Background: Zach's Journey Zach started coding at age 7 after his mom enrolled him in a coding camp because he was obsessed with video games and wanted to make his own.

For years, he built small games and side projects that went nowhere, but those years of tinkering gave him the foundational knowledge he needed when the right idea came along.

The idea came from personal frustration. Zach wanted to bulk up at the gym and started tracking calories using My Fitness Pal.

After just three days, he quit because the process of weighing every meal on a scale and manually typing out every ingredient was too tedious.

To him, every fitness app on the market was painful to use.

When AI models started becoming widely available, Zach saw the opportunity: what if you could just take a picture of your food and get the calories instantly, without typing or weighing anything?

He teamed up with a friend from coding camp and a 23-year-old founder who had already grown two other apps to millions of downloads.

Together, they built the first version of Cal AI in

about a month and launched it around May 2024. The app was dead simple but incredibly useful: press one button, snap a photo, and a prompt was sent to an AI model to calculate the calories.

Within 3 to 4 months, they hit a million downloads. After 6 months, they were generating over $1 million per month in revenue.

By early 2026, they sold the app to their biggest competitor for a reported nine figures.

Why This Matters Now When Zach built Cal AI, he already knew how to code, which gave him a real advantage. But the landscape has completely changed since he started.

In 2026, the barrier to building an app is essentially gone thanks to "vibe coding" tools, platforms that let you describe what you want in plain English and the AI builds the app for you.

Even Zach acknowledged this shift, pointing to a friend from high school who built an app called Wrestle AI (also known as Fight AI) using similar tools.

That app is now doing $30,000 per month, and the creator is just a regular college student.

Step 1: Find Your Idea This is the step most people get wrong before they even start building. The biggest mindset shift is understanding that you do not start with a product.

You start with a problem.

Zach did not sit down and say "I want to build a cool AI app." He experienced a specific, frustrating problem (tedious calorie tracking) and then built a simple version of the solution.

People don't download apps because they look good or because they have extra space on their phone.

They download apps because the app solves a very specific problem in their lives.

Examples of Problem-First Thinking ● Uber solved the problem of getting a ride without calling a cab and waiting 30 minutes. ● Duolingo solved the problem of language courses being too expensive and too boring. ● Cal AI solved the problem of tedious calorie weighing and manual tracking. Every successful app starts with one core feature that makes someone say, "I need that." How to Find Your Idea (Action Steps) 1. Start with your own life. What is a problem you have run into that frustrates you? How could you solve it?

2. Look at outdated solutions. My Fitness Pal had been around for over a decade. It was bloated, complicated, and had not innovated in years.

That created an opportunity to build something simpler and faster with AI.

Browse the top apps in different categories.

Try them out and ask: which ones feel old?

Which ones are doing something that AI could make 10x easier?

3. Build the simplest possible version. Use one of the AI tools listed above. Get a working prototype out as fast as possible.

Do not spend six months perfecting something that nobody has used yet.

4. Move fast. Speed matters because other people get the same ideas. Cal AI had a ton of copycats, but Cal AI won because they got into the market first and capitalized on their head start.

Step 2: Get People to See It (Marketing) Having a great product is only the first piece of the puzzle. Without marketing, nobody will know your app exists.

Zach put it bluntly: if you had a perfect app but the worst marketing, you would get no downloads.

If you had the worst app but amazing marketing, you would still get downloads and make some money.

Marketing beats building. Every time. What Cal AI Did Zach reinvested almost all of the revenue they made back into making sure people knew about the app.

His specific strategy was influencer-driven content that did not look like advertising.

The process: 1. Zach sent hundreds of DMs each day to fitness creators on TikTok and Instagram. At first, almost nobody responded, but he kept going until he found creators willing to work with him.

2. They did not ask influencers to do traditional sponsored content like "Hey guys, this video is sponsored by Cal AI." Instead, they told the creator to make their regular content and, somewhere in the first 15 seconds, show themselves using the app, simply snapping a picture of food and letting the calories pop up on screen.

3. Because the concept of scanning your food and instantly seeing calories is so visual, people watching the videos immediately noticed it and flooded the comments asking, "What app is that?"

Why This Works Traditional ads tell your brain to ignore them. But when a fitness creator you have been following for years casually scans their lunch, your brain does not register it as an ad.

It feels like a recommendation from a friend.

You don't trust a billboard, but when your friend says "Try this app," you download it that night.

How to Replicate This (Action Steps) 1. Find 20 to 30 creators in whatever market your app serves. They do not need to be huge. Even someone with a few thousand followers works.

2. DM them. Tell them what your app does and show them why their audience would care about it.

Offer a flat rate for a bundle of videos.

3. Give them creative freedom. They know what works with their audience. Just give them the app and tell them to show it in the first 15 seconds of the video.

The less it looks like an ad, the better it will perform.

4. Track everything. Look at when the video was posted, how many views it got, and what your downloads and revenue looked like on that same day.

This is how you figure out which influencers and types of content work best.

5. Double down on what works. Sign more creators who match the profile of your top performers and scale from there.

Step 3: Build Predictable Income (Retention) A lot of people think that once someone downloads your app, the deal is done. In reality, it is just starting.

Cal AI operated in a highly competitive market with many copycats.

Some months they could add thousands of new users; other months, only a few hundred.

To build predictable revenue, they needed every existing user to keep using the app.

If users downloaded Cal AI, used it for a week, and then switched to a competitor, the entire business model would fall apart.

Strategy 1: The Onboarding Funnel When you first open Cal AI, it asks a series of personal questions: What is your weight? What is your goal weight?

Why are you trying to lose or gain weight?

How will this improve your life?

This is intentional. When you ask personal questions about goals and motivations, users invest time and emotional energy before they even see the subscription page.

By the time someone has answered 10 questions about why they want to get healthier, paying $30 for the year feels like the obvious next step.

This leads to significantly higher conversion rates.

Strategy 2: Effortless Core Experience Cal AI made the app so simple that using it was basically effortless. You take a picture, you get your calories, and you are done.

The whole interaction takes maybe 5 seconds.

Compare that

to a traditional fitness app where you are scrolling through databases, searching for your food, putting it on a scale, and manually adjusting portions.

The key insight: The less effort it takes to use your app every day, the more likely people are to keep using it.

Strategy 3: Habit-Building Features Cal AI added small features that reinforce daily usage: daily streaks, simple dashboards showing your progress, and a health score that tells you how well you are eating.

These are not groundbreaking features, but they give users a reason to come back and check in every single day.

And if you are using something every single day, it becomes hard to justify switching to something else.

Why Retention Leads to Acquisition When your monthly revenue is solid and predictable, that is when your app becomes attractive to bigger companies and when acquisition offers start coming in.

Cal AI selling for nine figures proved that this model works.
