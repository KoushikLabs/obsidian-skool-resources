Objections "That's fake / not authentic" ● To an extent I agree. I never plan to have my YouTube channel be an AI avatar. Maybe if I made a new channel that was purely news-based as an experiment to see how well it could do being automated but I want my YouTube channel to feel personal.

● And I actually enjoy making YouTube videos. ● The script is 100% yours. The voice is yours. The face is yours. The only thing missing is you physically sitting in a

chair. "Won't this flood the internet with garbage content?" ● AI writing tools already exist and the best content still wins. ● Removing the production bottleneck means MORE content, but the quality filter is still the

idea. ● Bad content with a good avatar is still bad content. "Isn't this going to kill video editor jobs?" ● It changes the job, not eliminates it. Editors become AI orchestrators and quality reviewers.

A single video's VO session = $300-$1,000+. ● Studio recording session: $50-$200/hour for the room alone, plus talent/director fees. ● One professionally edited YouTube video with VO can cost $400-$1,000+. The entire AI stack costs less per month than a single human-produced video. The Right Way to Think About This This is not cheap in absolute terms. A few hundred bucks a month is real money. But the framing most people use is wrong. They compare the subscription cost to zero. The real comparison is: what's your time

worth? If this pipeline gives you back 10 hours a week of recording and editing time, that's 40 hours a month. At $251/month, you're paying $6.28/hour for your own time back. That's the real math. Claude Code at $200/month sounds expensive until you realize it's the most productive tool you own.
